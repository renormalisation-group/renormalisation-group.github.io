Three margin-note treatments. These are isolated previews; the article is unchanged.

01 Quiet sidenotes

Full references beside the passage, with a muted number and no divider or box.

Engels embodied this zeitgeist, this “enthusiasm of reason” wholly.6

He kept company with a number of scientists and read extensively on advances in the natural sciences and mathematics throughout his life.8

6 G. W. F. Hegel, The Philosophy of History (1840), p. 535.

8 Engels to Marx, 14 July 1858. MECW, vol. 40, pp. 325–327.

02 One active note

Select a citation number. The margin keeps only that reference in view.

Engels embodied this zeitgeist, this “enthusiasm of reason” wholly.

He kept company with a number of scientists and read extensively on advances in the natural sciences and mathematics throughout his life.

6 G. W. F. Hegel, The Philosophy of History (1840), p. 535.

8 Engels to Marx, 14 July 1858. MECW, vol. 40, pp. 325–327.

03 Brief citation, expandable detail

Short source labels in the margin. Open one to reveal its full reference.

Engels embodied this zeitgeist, this “enthusiasm of reason” wholly.6

He kept company with a number of scientists and read extensively on advances in the natural sciences and mathematics throughout his life.8

6 Hegel, Philosophy of History

G. W. F. Hegel, The Philosophy of History (1840), p. 535. Cited by Engels in Socialism: Utopian and Scientific.

8 Engels to Marx, 1858

Engels to Marx, 14 July 1858. Marx–Engels Collected Works, vol. 40, pp. 325–327.
